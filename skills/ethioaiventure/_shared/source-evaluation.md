# Source Evaluation

## Source Hierarchy

| Tier | Sources | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Government, regulatory authorities, official company sources, academic papers, research institutions, financial filings, official datasets | Authoritative for facts; prefer for consequential conclusions |
| Tier 2 | Reputable journalism, industry reports, analyst reports, credible expert research | Corroborate; good for context and leads |
| Tier 3 | Reddit, X, LinkedIn, forums, developer communities | Signals and sentiment only; never automatic authority |

## Evaluation Questions

For every important source ask:

1. Who published it, and what is their incentive to publish this?
2. Is it primary (original) or secondary (reporting on something)?
3. How close is the publisher to the claim being made?
4. What is the publication date — is this current for the question?
5. Does another independent source corroborate it?
6. What would make this source wrong, and is there evidence of that?

## Rules

1. Primary sources preferred for consequential conclusions.
2. Never assume lack of search results means lack of competition (absence of evidence is not evidence of absence).
3. Community sources may identify signals and sentiment but must not automatically be treated as authoritative evidence.
4. For time-sensitive claims, check the publication date before relying on the source.
5. In scheduled reports, retain per item: source, publication date, discovery date, source type, confidence, verification status.
6. If a claim cannot be sourced, say so: *"I could not verify this."*
