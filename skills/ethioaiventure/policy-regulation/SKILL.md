# Policy & Regulation

## When to Use
- AI/data/fintech/telecom/health regulation questions for Ethiopia or African markets
- Compliance assessment for a product or opportunity
- Policy signals in scheduled reports

## Procedure
1. Scope jurisdiction and topic:
   - Ethiopian regulation (AI, data protection, privacy, fintech, healthcare, telecom, cybersecurity, IP, digital identity)
   - African AI policy (continental + regional: AU, IGAD, EAC, ECOWAS, SADC)
   - Global precedents relevant to African markets
2. Use authoritative sources for consequential conclusions: gazettes, regulators (e.g., INSA, NBE, ECA, telecom authority), government publications, official policy documents, credible legal analysis.
3. Map what is law vs draft vs policy vs guidance. Note enforcement reality (what is actually enforced vs paper law).
4. Cross-border data, data localization, digital identity, licensing requirements, sector-specific approvals.
5. For a product: identify which regulations apply, what compliance costs, and what approvals are needed.
6. Classify evidence; state what could not be verified.

## Output Format
```text
Regulatory landscape (per topic: status, authority, key requirements, source)
Law vs draft vs guidance distinction
Enforcement reality
Implications for the opportunity/product (costs, approvals, risks)
Unknowns
```

## Pitfalls
- Treating draft law as law
- Ignoring enforcement reality (paper vs practice)
- Using a single secondary source for consequential regulatory claims

## Verification
- Consequential claims trace to authoritative sources
- Law/draft/guidance status explicit
- Enforcement gap noted
